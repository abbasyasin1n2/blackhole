🌌 Einstein's Field Equations

The foundation of black hole physics lies in Einstein's field equations of general relativity, which describe how matter and energy curve spacetime.

Einstein Field Equations
Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴)Tμν
Where Gμν is the Einstein tensor, Λ is the cosmological constant, gμν is the metric tensor, G is the gravitational constant, c is the speed of light, and Tμν is the stress-energy tensor.
Spacetime Curvature

Matter tells spacetime how to curve, and spacetime tells matter how to move. Black holes represent regions where spacetime curvature becomes infinite at the singularity.

⚫ Schwarzschild Solution

The Schwarzschild solution describes the spacetime geometry around a non-rotating, spherically symmetric black hole.

Schwarzschild Metric
ds² = -(1 - Rs/r)c²dt² + (1 - Rs/r)⁻¹dr² + r²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²)
Where Rs = 2GM/c² is the Schwarzschild radius, and (r, θ, φ) are spherical coordinates.

Schwarzschild Radius: 12.7 million km

Derivation of Schwarzschild Radius

Step 1: Start with Newtonian escape velocity vesc = √(2GM/r)
Step 2: Set escape velocity equal to speed of light: c = √(2GM/r)
Step 3: Solve for r: r = 2GM/c²
Step 4: This gives the Schwarzschild radius Rs = 2GM/c²

🌀 Kerr Solution (Rotating Black Holes)

Real black holes rotate, and the Kerr solution describes the spacetime around a rotating black hole.

Kerr Metric (Simplified)
ds² = -(1 - 2Mr/ρ²)dt² + (ρ²/Δ)dr² + ρ²dθ² + sin²θ(r² + a²cos²θ + 2Ma²rsin²θ/ρ²)dφ² - (4Mrasin²θ/ρ²)dtdφ
Where a = J/Mc is the angular momentum parameter, ρ² = r² + a²cos²θ, and Δ = r² - 2Mr + a².
Frame Dragging

A rotating black hole drags spacetime around with it, creating a region called the ergosphere where no object can remain stationary.

🌊 Gravitational Lensing

Black holes bend light rays, creating gravitational lensing effects that can be calculated using general relativity.

Light Deflection Angle
α = 4GM/(bc²) = 4GM/(bc²) × (1 + 15π/16 × GM/(bc²))
Where b is the impact parameter (closest approach distance). The second term is the post-Newtonian correction.

⚡ Hawking Radiation

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime predicts that black holes emit radiation due to virtual particle pairs near the event horizon.

Hawking Temperature
TH = ℏc³/(8πGMkB) = ℏ/(8πkBc) × c²/(GM)
Where ℏ is the reduced Planck constant, kB is Boltzmann's constant, and M is the black hole mass.
Black Hole Lifetime
τ = (5120πG²M³)/(ℏc⁴) ≈ 10⁶⁷ years × (M/M☉)³
The time for a black hole to completely evaporate through Hawking radiation.

🌌 Geodesic Equations

Particles and light rays follow geodesics (shortest paths) in curved spacetime. The geodesic equations for Schwarzschild spacetime are:

Radial Geodesic Equation
(dr/dτ)² = E²/c² - (1 - Rs/r)(1 + L²/(m²c²r²))
Where E is energy, L is angular momentum, m is mass, and τ is proper time.
Angular Geodesic Equation
d²φ/dτ² = -(2/r)(dr/dτ)(dφ/dτ)
This describes the precession of orbits due to general relativistic effects.
